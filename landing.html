How an alert fires

Four steps, and only one of them is yours.

01

Bars arrive in real time

A live stream feeds the one-, five-, fifteen-minute and hourly series for every symbol you watch.

02

Nothing is judged mid-candle

A rule only ever sees closed bars, so a wick that reverses before the close never wakes you.

03

It fires on the transition

The alert sends once, when the condition becomes true, and re-arms only after it clears again.

04

The email lands

Clean HTML mail from your own verified domain. No app to open, no tab to keep alive.

The number nobody else prints

We show you the lag instead of hiding it.

Every alerting tool says instant. The truth is that a rule cannot see a move until its bar closes — an average of half the timeframe, which dwarfs every other delay combined. Pick a timeframe and watch what you are actually signing up for.

7.5 min

15 min rule: about 7.5 minutes

Average wait for the bar boundary — half the timeframe. Evaluation and delivery add seconds on top. The dashboard derives this from your own rules; it is not a marketing figure.

What you get

Built by someone who got tired of watching tabs.

Dead-man's switch

If the evaluation loop stops completing healthy cycles you get an email, and the dashboard shows Monitor stalled. You find out when the monitor breaks — not when you miss a trade.

Zero-spam de-duplication

A transition-based state machine sends one alert when the condition turns true, then holds until it clears. An oversold hour is one email, not sixty.

Backtest before you arm it

Replay any condition over the recent lookback window and get the fire count, the fire rate, and the exact bars it would have triggered on.

Watchlist at a glance

Live price and RSI for every symbol across every timeframe, colour-coded oversold and overbought — before you commit to a rule.

Describe it in English

“Email me when NVDA's 5-minute RSI drops below 15.” An in-process model drafts the rule — no cloud, no API key. Optional, and it fails open.

Yours to run

MIT-licensed and self-hostable, with three runtime dependencies. Bring your own market-data key and mail domain, or use the hosted one.
